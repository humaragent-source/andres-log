Proposal for a New
MCC Classification for
Regulated Payment Stablecoins

Why MCC 6051 has become a categorization failure — and what a GENIUS-compliant successor looks like.
Thesis. The Merchant Category Code applied to stablecoin purchases — MCC 6051, "Quasi-Cash" — was designed in an era when stablecoins did not exist. It treats fully-reserved, federally-supervised, audited payment stablecoins with the same risk profile as gambling chips, money orders, and offshore wires. The result is auth rates near 65%, all-in user costs of 7–9%, and a categorization gap that the GENIUS Act now allows us to close. This document presents both an interim and an ideal MCC pathway, the empirical case, and a path to coalition action.

The empirical inversion. Stablecoin purchases through compliant on-ramps already exhibit lower fraud and lower chargebacks than standard e-commerce — yet they are categorized as higher-risk. The data argues for re-classification on operational grounds alone, before any regulatory change is invoked.
03 · GENIUS Act Regulatory Grounding

The Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for U.S. Stablecoins Act (signed July 2025; effective January 2027) creates the first federal category of permitted payment stablecoin issuer (PPSI). This category is the legal foundation that makes recategorization defensible.

  • Federal supervision. PPSIs are supervised by the OCC (federal) or qualifying state regulators with substantial similarity. This is bank-grade oversight, not money-services-business oversight.
  • 1:1 reserve mandate. Permitted payment stablecoins must hold reserves in cash, T-bills, or equivalent HQLA. Monthly attestation. This is closer to a money-market fund than to "quasi-cash."
  • Redemption rights. Holders have a statutory right to redeem at par. Removes the principal risk that justifies elevated MCC treatment.
  • BSA / AML obligations. PPSIs are themselves financial institutions under the Bank Secrecy Act, with full KYC, transaction monitoring, and SAR-filing obligations. AML risk is addressed at the issuer layer.

The implication is direct. If a stablecoin is issued by a federally-supervised PPSI, holds bank-quality reserves, is redeemable at par, and is itself a BSA-regulated financial institution, then a purchase of that stablecoin from a licensed on-ramp is — by every meaningful measure — lower-risk than a typical e-commerce transaction. The MCC framework simply has not caught up.

04 · Two-Track MCC Proposal

We propose a phased path that delivers operational relief in months while pursuing the durable structural fix in parallel. Both tracks are technically achievable within ISO 18245 governance and existing Visa / Mastercard rule-change procedures.

Track 1 · Interim · 0–6 Months
Sub-Classification Within Existing MCCs
PROPOSED: MCC 6051-S "Permitted Stablecoin Issuer Purchase" or MCC 6012-S routing under Financial Institutions
Network-level rule change directing issuer banks to apply standard purchase risk treatment (no cash-advance fee, standard interchange tier, no mandatory step-up auth) when the merchant of record is a registered PPSI under GENIUS or its on-ramp partner is sponsored by a chartered bank acquirer (e.g., Erebor). Implementable via Visa / Mastercard operating regulation update — does not require ISO 18245 change. Target: a pilot live in Q4 2026, ahead of the GENIUS effective date.
Track 2 · Ideal · 12–24 Months
A New, Purpose-Built MCC
PROPOSED: MCC 6XXX "Regulated Digital Asset Issuer — Payment Stablecoin"
New ISO 18245 classification, ratified by Visa, Mastercard, and the ISO maintenance committee, applicable only to merchants that are (a) registered PPSIs under GENIUS, or (b) on-ramps with bank-sponsored acquiring routing exclusively to PPSI-issued stablecoins. Eligibility tied to a published whitelist maintained by the networks. Provides permanent, durable, globally portable risk categorization aligned with the regulatory reality.
05 · Risk Analysis — Anticipating The Pushback
Concern: Fraud risk

PPSI purchases through licensed on-ramps already show ~0.10% fraud — below the 0.22% baseline for standard e-commerce. KYC at the on-ramp plus PPSI-level monitoring creates two layers of fraud screening, more than typical card-not-present commerce.

Concern: Chargeback exposure

Sub-0.2% chargeback rate vs. 0.6% e-commerce baseline. The asset is delivered immediately on-chain and is verifiable; "item not received" disputes are mechanically refutable, reducing legitimate chargeback grounds.

Concern: AML & sanctions risk

PPSIs are BSA-regulated financial institutions; the on-ramp is an MTL-licensed MSB with its own AML program. Both file SARs, maintain OFAC screening, and are subject to FinCEN supervision. AML risk is addressed at two regulated layers.

Concern: Asset volatility

PPSI stablecoins are 1:1 reserved with HQLA, attested monthly, and carry statutory redemption rights. Volatility is by definition not a risk vector — this is precisely what GENIUS was designed to eliminate.

The risk thesis, summarized. Every legacy concern that justified MCC 6051 — volatility, opacity, AML uncertainty, fraud propensity — has been structurally addressed by the GENIUS Act's PPSI framework and by the data observable in licensed on-ramp transaction flow today. The proposal is not asking the networks to take new risk. It is asking them to recognize a category that no longer carries the risk profile of its parent code.
