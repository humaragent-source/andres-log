Coastal Grade

A region-first grade of beach towns that share a workday with California.

I think we are heading into a world of abundance. Elon Musk has been saying AI will do work that used to take a building full of people, then robots will make the physical stuff, and money starts to matter less because food, housing, transport, and entertainment are just there. The first half I buy. The miss is the land. Earth is about 149 million square kilometers, and almost all of it you can put a house on. The ice-free shore is on the order of 730,000 kilometers. A third of all coastline is Arctic. Only about a third of what is left is even a beach. Year-round warm, inhabitable beach, with mountains that catch water and storms that are not a death sentence, is a rounding error. That is the land people will still bid on. You cannot print it. The version of that I care about is beachfront Mexico on the same clock as California: one flight, same workday, a life that is several times the house you could buy in the Bay. Puerto Vallarta, Punta Mita, and Sayulita already proved the demand. The hard part is the towns around them. Which ones have the same characteristics, the same kind of runway, and a price that still has room.

I built Coastal Grade because starting listing by listing is how you get lost. You need a high-level read of the whole region first. The app compares towns around Puerto Vallarta on one Coastal Grade, 0 to 100: asking price against towns I already like, drive from the airport, safety, drinkable water, the beach, storm history, and a pipeline of what is being built. Growth is relative. A town looks different once you sit it next to the ones around it. Hotels are a tell. If a Ritz-Carlton or a Fairmont is going in, that pocket is being bet on. I pull those announcements so you can see which towns have more room than the ones next to them, before you look at a house. I made it to decide for myself. If other people want it, I can share it. It might work for someone else, in this region or another one, based on what they actually care about.
